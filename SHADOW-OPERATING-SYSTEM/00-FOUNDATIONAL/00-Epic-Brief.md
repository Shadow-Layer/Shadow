# SHADOW Epic Brief

## Summary

**SHADOW** is a decentralised operating system for a collective of digital professionals (developers, designers, researchers, engineers) who collaborate on software projects, applications, and open-source work while maintaining anonymity and professional autonomy.

SHADOW enables members to:
- **Gain real-world experience and build portfolios** without exposing personal identity
- **Contribute to projects** either collaboratively (with revenue potential) or credibility-focused (portfolio only)
- **Lead projects** they originate without central approval
- **Earn reputation and professional references** through verifiable work history

SHADOW itself operates as a **revenue-generating venture**, launching with two core applications (Student Housing Archive, Tenga Inventory POS) that generate subscription revenue to fund operations and commons infrastructure.

---

## Context & Problem

### Current Landscape

Digital professionals (especially in emerging markets) often face:
- **Constrained portfolio options** — difficulty building credible work history without personal exposure
- **Anonymity vs. credibility tradeoff** — you can't be anonymous *and* get professional references from the same entity
- **Freelance fragmentation** — scattered across platforms with no persistent collective identity
- **No institutional memory** — work experience doesn't accumulate into collective reputation

### SHADOW's Position

SHADOW solves this by creating a **collective with persistent identity**, while members maintain personal privacy.

**The innovation:** SHADOW itself becomes known for shipping professional software. Members contribute. The collective's reputation grows. Individual members earn portable reputation *within that ecosystem* without exposing legal identity to the broader world.

## Who Is Affected

### Primary Users

**SHADOW Members** — Digital professionals seeking:
- Real paid project work (revenue-share opportunities)
- Portfolio-building contributions (credibility without payment)
- Collaboration with vetted peers
- Professional references (verified by SHADOW)
- Experience that travels with them if they leave

### Secondary Stakeholders

**SHADOW Commons** — The founding team running:
- Core revenue applications (housing archive, inventory POS)
- Collective infrastructure
- Member onboarding/credentialing
- Project governance
- Reputation & reference systems

**Project Leaders** — SHADOW members who originate and lead projects, controlling scope, team, and resource allocation

**External Clients/Users** — End users of SHADOW's applications and client projects

## Why This Work Matters Now

1. **Decentralisation trend** — DAOs, cooperatives, and network models are becoming operationally viable with proper architecture
2. **Anonymity demand** — Privacy-conscious professionals are actively seeking alternatives to name-brand portfolios
3. **Revenue viability** — SHADOW has concrete commercial products (housing archive, POS) providing immediate revenue model
4. **Scalability gap** — Existing models (freelance platforms, open-source collectives) don't handle revenue-sharing, governance, or professional references at scale
5. **First-mover advantage** — The specific combination (anonymous + collaborative + revenue-generating + professionally credible) doesn't exist yet

## Scope: In / Out

### IN SCOPE

- **Organisational architecture** — Decentralised governance, autonomy, no single point of failure
- **Membership system** — Identity layering (legal/verified/project/public), anonymity by default
- **Project sovereignty** — Team Leaders have operational authority over their projects
- **Economic system** — Commitments (voluntary/paid/revenue-share), compensation models, equity
- **Reputation without identity** — How members build verifiable work history without exposing legal identity
- **SHADOW Commons** — Revenue applications, central infrastructure, cross-project coordination
- **Financial/legal architecture** — Contracts, IP ownership, taxation, entity structures
- **Security & privacy** — Identity management, access controls, incident response
- **Scalability** — How the system functions at 10, 50, 250, 1,000, 10,000+ members
- **Resilience** — Succession, disaster recovery, handling founder/leader departure

### OUT OF SCOPE (for now)

- Detailed UI/UX design for any SHADOW platforms
- Specific technology stack decisions
- Marketing/growth strategy
- Detailed legal jurisdiction-specific advice (flagged where needed, but not drafted)
- Detailed project scope for the two revenue apps (housing archive, POS)

## Key Design Tensions

The architecture must navigate these deliberately:

| Tension | Extremes | SHADOW Approach |
|---------|----------|-----------------|
| **Anonymity vs. Accountability** | Hide identity completely / Expose everything | Layered identity: anonymous within SHADOW, verifiable where needed (contracts, legal), pseudonymous for reputation |
| **Autonomy vs. Coordination** | Every team leads independently / Central control dictates all | Team Leaders are sovereign within projects; Commons handles cross-project coordination & shared infrastructure only |
| **Voluntary work vs. Viability** | Pure volunteering kills projects / Everyone must be paid | Support three commitment types: voluntary (portfolio), paid (project revenue), revenue-share (venture upside) |
| **Scalability without bureaucracy** | No central rules / Bureaucratic nightmarescape | Decentralise all project decisions; centralise only standards, identity, and legal compliance |
| **Revenue concentration vs. Commons health** | SHADOW takes nothing / SHADOW takes everything | SHADOW takes substantial cut from revenue projects; uses this to fund core apps and commons infrastructure |

---

## Foundational Principles

1. **Project Sovereignty** — Whoever originates a project becomes Team Leader with operational authority
2. **Anonymity by Default** — Personal identity disclosed only where legally/contractually necessary
3. **Voluntary Commitment** — Membership ≠ obligation; every commitment must be explicit
4. **Agreement Principle** — All participation governed by clear, pre-agreed terms
5. **Minimum Centralisation** — SHADOW Commons exists only to handle genuinely shared concerns
6. **No Single Point of Failure** — Architecture survives loss of founders, Team Leaders, infrastructure
7. **Reputation ≠ Authority** — Demonstrated competence doesn't automatically grant control

---

## Success Criteria

The architecture succeeds if it enables:

- ✓ A 5-person founding team to bootstrap and operate SHADOW
- ✓ Projects to exist independently without asking central permission
- ✓ Members to contribute under various commitment types without confusion
- ✓ Clear resolution when Team Leaders disappear or conflict arises
- ✓ SHADOW revenue apps to function and generate subscription income
- ✓ Individual contributors to build verifiable portfolios without exposing legal identity
- ✓ Scaling to 250+ members without proportional bureaucratic overhead
- ✓ Survival if a founder disappears or core infrastructure fails
- ✓ Legal viability across jurisdictions where projects operate
