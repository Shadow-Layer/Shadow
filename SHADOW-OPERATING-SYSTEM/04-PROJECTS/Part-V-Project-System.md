# SHADOW Operating System — Part V: Project System

**Project Formation, Leadership, Lifecycle & Succession**

---

## 1. Project Creation

### 1.1 Project Originator Principle

**Any verified SHADOW member may create a project.**

The member who originates a project becomes its **Team Leader** unless they explicitly designate another member.

**No approval required.**

The Commons is not consulted. Members do not vote. The originator simply:

1. Fills out Project Charter template
2. Announces project to SHADOW
3. Begins recruiting team
4. Starts work

**Authority granted to originator:** Full operational control of project.

---

### 1.2 Project Charter

**Every project creates a Project Charter at inception.**

**Minimum contents:**

- **Project ID** — Unique identifier (e.g., "PROJ-2026-001-HOUSING-APP")
- **Project Name** — Clear, descriptive name
- **Originating Member** — Who created it
- **Team Leader** — Who leads it (usually originator, can be different)
- **Objective** — What problem does this solve? What are we building?
- **Scope** — What's included/excluded
- **Expected Deliverables** — What will exist at completion
- **Team** — Initial team members (can grow)
- **Roles** — What role does each person have
- **Commitment Type** — Volunteer / Paid / Revenue-share / Other
- **Compensation Model** — If paid, how much and when?
- **IP Ownership** — Who owns the work created
- **Confidentiality Level** — SHADOW-PUBLIC / INTERNAL / PROJECT-CONFIDENTIAL / RESTRICTED
- **Expected Duration** — Timeline (3 months? 1 year? Ongoing?)
- **Decision Authority** — Who decides what in this project
- **Exit Rights** — How can members leave
- **Successor Designation** — If Team Leader leaves, who takes over
- **Resource Needs** — Any Commons resources needed (hosting, tools, money?)
- **Legal Obligations** — Any contracts, NDAs, regulatory requirements

**Charter is binding.** It becomes the project's operating agreement.

## 2. Project Lifecycle

### 2.1 Stages

```
IDEA (concept in member's head)
  ↓
PROPOSAL (shared with potential team)
  ↓
FORMATION (team recruited, charter signed)
  ↓
ACTIVE (work in progress)
  ├──── PAUSED (temporary hold)
  ├──── TRANSFERRED (leadership changes)
  │
  ↓
COMPLETED (deliverables finished)
  ↓
ARCHIVED (historical record)
```

---

### 2.2 Stage Details

**IDEA**
- Member has project concept
- Not yet announced to SHADOW
- Not yet requiring team

**PROPOSAL**
- Project proposed to potential team
- Charter in draft form
- Recruiting team members
- Getting feedback

**FORMATION**
- Team has agreed to participate
- Charter is finalised and documented
- Roles are assigned
- Resources are allocated (if needed)
- Ready to work

**ACTIVE**
- Work is happening
- Team is meeting deadlines
- Progress is tracked
- Status updates submitted to Commons (monthly or quarterly)

**PAUSED**
- Project is temporarily halted
- Reasons: funding, resource availability, team circumstances, etc.
- Can be resumed with agreement from active team
- Status maintained (reputation, portfolio)

**TRANSFERRED**
- Leadership changes (old Team Leader leaves, new Team Leader takes over)
- Project charter is updated
- Same project continues under new leadership
- Reputation is continuous

**COMPLETED**
- Deliverables are finished
- Final review and handoff
- Project closes (team may move to new projects)
- Portfolio credit is recorded

**ARCHIVED**
- Project is historical record
- No longer active
- Contributors' work is permanently credited
- Can be referenced but not resumed
- Serves as institutional memory

## 3. Team Leader Authority & Responsibility

### 3.1 What Team Leaders Control

**Team Leaders have final authority to decide:**

- Project objective and scope
- Who joins the team (recruitment)
- Who leaves the team (removal)
- Role assignments
- Work prioritization and deadlines
- Technical approaches
- Quality standards
- Internal processes and meetings
- Project rules and confidentiality
- Whether to pause or end project
- Resource allocation (if team budget exists)
- Project representation (to clients, external parties)

**Team Leaders do NOT control:**

- Members' personal time or availability
- Work outside project scope
- Members' other projects
- Pre-existing member intellectual property
- Members' personal life or identity
- SHADOW-wide policies or governance

---

### 3.2 Team Leader Accountability

**Team Leaders are accountable for:**

- Project delivery (meeting defined objectives)
- Team member treatment (fairness, respect, no abuse)
- Accurate contribution records (who did what)
- Budget management (if funds are allocated)
- Security and confidentiality (protecting sensitive information)
- Legal compliance (contracts, data protection, etc.)
- Honest communication (with team and SHADOW)
- Succession planning (designating next Team Leader)

**Accountability mechanism:**

- Arbitration (if team member files complaint)
- Removal from project (by Commons if serious misconduct)
- Financial liability (if breach of contract)
- Reputation impact (visible in portfolio)

---

### 3.3 Team Leader Charter

**Every Team Leader receives a formal Charter:**

**You are responsible for:**

1. **Project Clarity**
   - Clear objective everyone understands
   - Written scope (what's in/out)
   - Defined deliverables
   - Realistic timeline

2. **Team Care**
   - Treat contributors fairly
   - Respect agreements
   - Pay on time (if paid project)
   - Respond to concerns/issues
   - Provide support and feedback
   - Protect privacy and security

3. **Documentation**
   - Project charter (maintained, updated if needed)
   - Contribution records (who did what)
   - Meeting notes (if applicable)
   - Decision log (important decisions)
   - Status updates (to Commons)

4. **Integrity**
   - Honest communication
   - Accurate credits
   - Respect IP ownership
   - No favoritism or discrimination
   - No hidden arrangements or coercion

5. **Escalation**
   - Report critical issues to Commons
   - Address misconduct or safety concerns
   - Seek arbitration if disputes arise
   - Request legal support if needed

**If you breach this charter:**
- Members can file complaint with Commons
- Arbitration panel will investigate
- Possible outcomes: warning, project oversight, removal as Team Leader
- Serious violations can result in SHADOW membership suspension

## 4. Contributor Relationships

### 4.1 Contributor Agreement

**Every project contributor signs a simple Contributor Agreement specifying:**

- **Commitment Type**
  - Volunteer (unpaid, portfolio-building)
  - Hourly (paid per hour)
  - Fixed-fee (paid for defined deliverable)
  - Revenue-share (paid % of project revenue)
  - Equity (ownership stake in project outcomes)
  - Fellowship/grant (special arrangement)

- **Compensation** (if applicable)
  - Amount
  - Payment schedule
  - Currency (if international)
  - Any conditions

- **Duration**
  - Expected timeframe
  - Whether ongoing or finite
  - Notice period for exit

- **Role & Responsibilities**
  - What they'll do
  - Expected hours/time commitment
  - Deliverables they own
  - Who they report to

- **Confidentiality**
  - What information is confidential
  - Who can access it
  - How long it stays confidential
  - Exceptions (legal disclosure, etc.)

- **Intellectual Property**
  - Who owns work created
  - Any pre-existing IP they bring
  - Licensing (if applicable)
  - Attribution (how credit is given)

- **Exit Terms**
  - How to leave the project
  - Notice period
  - Work-in-progress handling
  - Final compensation/payment
  - Post-project obligations

- **Dispute Resolution**
  - How disagreements are handled
  - Escalation to Commons/arbitration
  - Who pays for arbitration

**Contributor agreement is binding** and enforceable through arbitration.

---

### 4.2 Right to Leave

**A contributor may leave a project at any time.**

**Notice:** 
- For volunteer work: 3 days notice (good faith)
- For paid work: 2 weeks notice (standard)
- For equity/revenue-share: per agreement (typically 30 days)

**Upon leaving:**
- Contributor responsibilities end (except ongoing obligations)
- Portfolio credit is recorded (permanent)
- Final compensation is paid
- Access to project systems is revoked
- Confidentiality obligations continue (even after leaving)

**Team Leader cannot:**
- Prevent departure
- Seize work created by contributor
- Deny portfolio credit
- Retaliate or punish

**Dispute:** If contributor claims unpaid compensation or unearned portfolio credit, arbitration resolves it.

## 5. Project Portfolio Integration

### 5.1 Reputation & Portfolio Building

**Every project contributes to member reputation.**

**What's recorded:**
- Project name and link
- Role (what did member do?)
- Duration (when were they involved?)
- Key accomplishments
- Skills demonstrated
- Recommendation from Team Leader (optional)
- Link to public work (if applicable)

**Visibility:**
- Contributor chooses visibility (private to SHADOW vs. public portfolio)
- Public portfolio shows: project name, role, general accomplishment, skills
- Public portfolio does NOT show: internal project details, financial info, sensitive work
- Contributor can opt out specific projects from public display

---

### 5.2 Portfolio Disputes

**If contributor and Team Leader disagree on portfolio credit:**

**Contributor claims:**
- Team Leader is denying credit for work they did
- Team Leader is not documenting the role accurately
- Team Leader is taking credit for work they didn't do

**Process:**
1. Contributor and Team Leader attempt to resolve (discuss directly)
2. If unresolved, escalate to Commons with evidence:
   - Contributor provides their record of what they did
   - Team Leader provides counter-evidence
   - Commons reviews project documentation (code, files, meeting notes, etc.)
3. Arbitration panel makes binding decision
4. Portfolio is updated per decision

**Outcome:** Accurate credit is restored; repeated misattribution can result in Team Leader removal.

---

## 6. Project Succession

### 6.1 Team Leader Transition

**Every project has a designated successor.**

**Succession plan:**
- Primary Team Leader is named
- Successor/Deputy is named
- Succession plan is documented in charter

**If Team Leader leaves voluntarily:**
- Team Leader provides 2 weeks notice (or per agreement)
- Successor is activated
- Team continues under new leadership
- Project charter is updated
- Reputation is continuous (no break)

**If Team Leader disappears/becomes unavailable:**
- Successor assumes leadership (immediately)
- Commons is notified
- Project status is communicated to team
- Continuation is reviewed (team can decide to end project)

**If no successor was designated:**
- Commons appoints temporary Team Leader
- Team votes on permanent successor (if project to continue)
- Emergency governance procedures apply

---

### 6.2 Leadership Disputes

**If team disagrees with new Team Leader:**

- Team can appeal to Commons
- Commons facilitates mediation
- If no resolution, team can vote to replace Team Leader (70% vote)
- If team votes to end project, project is closed

---

## 7. Project Financial Management

### 7.1 Revenue Projects

**If a project has external funding (client pays):**

**Money flow:**
- Client pays project (often to Team Leader or Commons-managed account)
- Team Leader distributes per Contributor Agreement
- Team and Team Leader take their shares
- If Commons is involved (opted into revenue-share), Commons takes agreed %

**Budget visibility:**
- Contributor Agreement specifies compensation
- Project dashboard shows payments received (amount, date, client)
- Ledger is updated regularly
- Disputes handled via arbitration

---

### 7.2 Volunteer Projects

**If a project is unpaid (portfolio-building):**

**Expectations:**
- Contributors understand no payment
- "Payment" is portfolio credit
- Clear understanding of time commitment (estimated hours/week)
- Can pivot to paid project if opportunity arises

---

## 8. Project Confidentiality

### 8.1 Classification

**Each project sets confidentiality level:**

- **SHADOW-PUBLIC** — Can discuss outside SHADOW, reference in portfolio
- **INTERNAL** — Can discuss with SHADOW members, cannot share outside
- **PROJECT-CONFIDENTIAL** — Restricted to project team; even other SHADOW members can't know details
- **RESTRICTED** — Ultra-sensitive; limited access even within team

---

### 8.2 Enforcement

**Violations:**
- Accidental disclosure (first offense): warning + education
- Deliberate sharing: formal investigation → arbitration → sanctions
- Serious breach (e.g., client confidential data shared publicly): immediate removal, potential legal action

---

## 9. Project Closure

### 9.1 Normal Closure

**When project reaches completion:**

1. **Final deliverable** — Team delivers final work
2. **Quality review** — Client or stakeholder approves
3. **Portfolio update** — Final contribution records logged
4. **Financial settlement** — Final payments made
5. **Archival** — Project documentation stored
6. **Status change** — Project marked COMPLETED → ARCHIVED

---

### 9.2 Premature Closure

**If project ends before completion:**

1. **Decision** — Team Leader decides to end, or team votes (if Team Leader absent)
2. **Notification** — Team is informed; SHADOW is notified
3. **Work-in-progress handling** — Who takes what; what remains with project
4. **Financial settlement** — Unpaid compensation handled; milestone disputes resolved via arbitration
5. **Portfolio** — Partial contributions credited; project marked COMPLETED (with caveat)
6. **Lessons learned** — Optional postmortem to understand what happened

---

## 10. Project Interdependence

### 10.1 Shared Resources

**If two projects share a resource:**

- **Shared member** — Person works on multiple projects (allowed; each project has separate agreement)
- **Shared infrastructure** — Both use Commons hosting (Commons manages conflicts)
- **Shared IP** — Both reference same codebase or design (ownership must be clear)
- **Shared funding** — Both funded from same client (agreements specify)

**Problem resolution:**
- Projects attempt to resolve directly
- If conflict, escalate to Commons
- Commons arbitrates resource allocation

---

### 10.2 Cross-Project Dependencies

**If Project A depends on Project B:**

- **Agreement** — A and B document dependency
- **Timeline** — Clear when B must deliver to A
- **Change management** — If B changes scope/timeline, A is notified
- **Escalation** — If B misses delivery, dispute goes to arbitration (not Commons order)

---

## 11. Project Types

### 11.1 Portfolio Projects

**Purpose:** Member builds experience and portfolio

**Characteristics:**
- No external client
- Typically unpaid (volunteer)
- Commons infrastructure used (if requested)
- Contribution is portfolio-building
- IP owned by project (or per agreement)
- Commons may take portfolio (no revenue cut)

**Example:** Building an open-source tool, design system, research paper

---

### 11.2 Revenue Projects

**Purpose:** Generate income for team

**Characteristics:**
- External client (or internal revenue source)
- Money flows from client to team
- Compensation per Contributor Agreement
- Optional Commons involvement (fee-based for insurance/arbitration)
- IP per project agreement (often client owns deliverable)
- Portfolio credit given to contributors

**Example:** Client hires team to build software, design website, conduct research

---

### 11.3 Commons Projects

**Purpose:** Generate revenue for SHADOW operations

**Characteristics:**
- Created and owned by Commons
- Examples: Housing Archive app, Tenga Inventory POS
- Contributors hired with compensation agreements
- SHADOW retains all IP and revenue
- Contributors build portfolio + get paid
- Funds Commons operations, arbitration, infrastructure

**Example:** SHADOW's SaaS applications generating subscription revenue

---

## Summary

SHADOW's project system enables:

✓ Any member can originate and lead projects  
✓ Clear operational authority (Team Leader decides project direction)  
✓ Explicit agreements (every commitment documented upfront)  
✓ Accurate reputation/portfolio building (contributions permanently credited)  
✓ Succession planning (projects survive Team Leader departure)  
✓ Dispute resolution (arbitration if conflicts arise)  
✓ Financial transparency (money flow is visible)  
✓ Contributor protection (cannot be exploited; arbitration available)  
✓ Confidentiality respect (project secrecy maintained)  
✓ Scalability (same model works at 5 projects or 500 projects)  

Projects are autonomous operating units; SHADOW provides infrastructure and arbitration, not command.
